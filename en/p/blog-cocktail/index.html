Preface#

To be honest, although I have been to quite a few bars, I do not know much about cocktails, and my interest in them is fairly limited. I have tried making a few myself, but in the end I still found that I prefer my spirits neat 😮‍💨.

For this article, I used GPT to help organize the history and recipes. The rest records my own impressions of tasting cocktails and visiting cocktail bars.

What is a cocktail?#

Today, a cocktail usually means a drink made with one or more spirits and other ingredients. The word originally had a much narrower definition.

On May 13, 1806, the American newspaper The Balance and Columbian Repository published an editor’s answer to a reader’s question: a cock-tail was a “stimulating liquor” made with spirits, sugar, water, and bitters, also known as a “bittered sling.” This is one of the most frequently cited early printed definitions that survive; it does not mean cocktails were invented on that date. Virginia Tech University Libraries also notes a possible earlier use in 1803, though the relevant newspaper has not survived. 1

Those four ingredients still help explain an Old Fashioned: the spirit provides the base, sugar adjusts the sweetness, bitters add aroma and bitterness, and water and ice provide dilution. The similar structure alone does not prove that today’s Old Fashioned has been passed down unchanged from the 1806 recipe.

The roles inside a recipe#

Not every drink needs every role. A Martini centers on spirit and vermouth. A Daiquiri builds a sour-sweet balance from rum, lime, and sugar. A Negroni gives equal standing to gin, sweet vermouth, and a bitter aperitivo. Balance does not mean equal amounts of every flavor; it means each ingredient can be noticed without drowning out the others.

How to Find a Ratio and Make a Cocktail#

Start with a Reliable Recipe#

A cocktail name does not correspond to one fixed ratio. Start by searching for its English name in a reliable source such as the IBA and noting each ingredient and quantity. The recipe gives you a starting point you can reproduce, though not every bar will use the same version. 2

Scale the Recipe, Then Adjust to Taste#

Take the IBA Daiquiri as an example: 60 ml white rum, 20 ml lime juice, and two bar spoons of superfine sugar. The ratio of base spirit to acid is about 3:1, with sugar to adjust the sweetness. For half a serving, halve everything: 30 ml, 10 ml, and one bar spoon. 5

  1. Measure according to the recipe first. Use a jigger, and do not change the proportions by eye on your first attempt.
  2. Scale all ingredients proportionally. For two drinks, double the liquid quantities and adjust small amounts of sugar or bitters accordingly.
  3. Taste, then change one thing at a time. If it is too sour, add a little syrup; if it is too sweet, add a little citrus juice. If the alcohol tastes too strong, check how much the ice has melted and diluted the drink. Record your adjustments so you can reproduce them.

Choose a Method That Fits the Ingredients#

  • Shake: Drinks containing juice, egg white, or cream are often shaken with ice to chill and combine the ingredients quickly. Add carbonated ingredients such as soda water or Champagne after shaking.
  • Stir: Spirit-forward drinks such as the Old Fashioned and Negroni can be stirred gently with ice in a mixing glass and then strained into a serving glass. This chills and dilutes the drink while keeping it clear. 36
  • Build: For long drinks such as a highball, measure the spirit into an ice-filled serving glass, add soda water or ginger ale, and stir gently.
  • Blend: For frozen or tropical drinks, follow the recipe and blend the ingredients with crushed ice. The IBA Zombie is one example that uses a blender. 2

Three classics, three structures#

Old Fashioned: let the base spirit lead#

The IBA recipe calls for 45 ml bourbon or rye whiskey, one sugar cube, a few drops of Angostura bitters, and a little water. Dissolve the sugar in the glass with the bitters and water, add ice, then pour in the whiskey and stir gently. Garnish with an orange slice or peel. 3

There are only a few ingredients, and the whiskey is not hidden by juice or soda. For more sweetness, adjust the sugar; for a softer drink, stir a little longer to let more ice melt. Changing one variable at a time makes it easier to tell what caused the difference.

Daiquiri: rum held in balance by lime and sugar#

The IBA Daiquiri uses 60 ml Cuban white rum, 20 ml fresh lime juice, and two bar spoons of superfine sugar. Stir until the sugar dissolves, add ice and shake, then strain into a chilled cocktail glass. 5

This is more than rum with juice: the lime’s acidity, the sugar’s sweetness, and the rum’s own flavor all need to work together. Follow the recipe the first time, then adjust the sugar to suit the acidity of the lime and your own taste.

Negroni: bitterness can lead#

The IBA recipe calls for 30 ml each of gin, Campari, and sweet red vermouth. Pour the ingredients into a glass filled with ice, stir gently, and garnish with half an orange slice. 6

This drink is a reminder that cocktails do not have to win you over with sweetness and acidity. Bitterness can take the lead, supported by herbal notes, citrus, and body. If you are not used to bitter flavors, try a small amount first or choose a drink with a higher proportion of soda water.

These are the versions listed by the International Bartenders Association (IBA), and they offer a starting point you can reproduce. Bars and bartenders may use different brands, proportions, and garnishes, so drinks with the same name will not necessarily taste identical.

If you do not drink alcohol, you can still use black tea, oolong tea, juice, herbs, and soda water to make an alcohol-free drink and explore the interplay of acidity, sweetness, bitterness, and aroma. Simply removing the base spirit from a recipe will not usually produce the same flavor or texture.

Cocktail flavors are worth exploring slowly, but alcohol itself is not a health drink. The World Health Organization says current evidence cannot identify an amount of alcohol with no health risk; drinking less lowers the risk. 10 There is no need to start drinking for the sake of cocktails, and no one should drive after drinking.

Personal Reflections#

I have had Long Island Iced Teas costing 30–40 yuan a glass, and I have also tried signature cocktails made by skilled bartenders. For me, one measure of a cocktail is how well the alcohol works with the other flavors: even at 20–30% ABV, it can be refreshing on the palate, taste almost like a soft drink, and leave no strong alcohol taste on the finish. A drink like that feels well made to me. Of course, some cocktails are meant to have a prominent spirit flavor, and whether you like that comes down to personal taste.

I have only been to quiet bars, so my observations are limited to those. In my experience, they offer more than drinks: there is also the atmosphere and a little space to be left alone. People talk among themselves and usually do not disturb one another; couples and friends make up much of the crowd.

Cocktail bars are usually not cheap. Some cocktails taste like soft drinks even when their alcohol content is not low, so if you do not know your limits, it can be easy to drink too much without realizing it.

Conclusion#

If you are at a bar and do not know what to order, try starting with a base spirit you like. If your budget allows, you could also try one of the bar’s signature cocktails. If you still cannot decide, sparkling water is a good choice too. In a quiet space, finding a flavor you enjoy and a pace that feels comfortable matters more than ordering a drink out of obligation.

By the way, my favorite spirit is whisky, and I do not need it in a cocktail: neat is just fine. Bar talk is always welcome. 🥃
