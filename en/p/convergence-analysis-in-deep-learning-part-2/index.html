0x00 Preface#

Given a starting point and a step size, I’d want to know how many steps I’m in for. The formulas from the last post1 are enough to start working that out. We’ll keep the Euclidean norm, using GD for smooth functions and subgradient steps for non-smooth ones.

Here’s where the iterates are allowed to go. The smooth proofs are unconstrained. For the non-smooth cases we can work on a closed convex set $\mathcal C$ and project back to it after each step. Assume a minimizer $w^{\ast}$ exists and write $f^{\ast}=f(w^{\ast})$.

0x01 Definition of Convergence#

Two people can say a method has converged and be looking at different numbers. Are you watching how much loss is left, where the parameter has gone, or how small the gradient is? Let’s write down those quantities first.

  • For the gap between the current objective value and the minimum, look at $f(w_T)-f^{\ast}$.
  • For the distance left in parameter space, look at $\Vert w_T-w^{\ast}\Vert^2$. With several minimizers, we can also measure distance to the whole set of solutions.
  • In the non-convex case we often look for a point with a small gradient, for example by bounding $\min_{0\le t<T}\Vert\nabla f(w_t)\Vert^2$ or averaging the squared norms.

That’s why I look at the left-hand side when someone shows me a bound. A small gradient means the point nearly satisfies first-order stationarity. If you’re asking how far the loss is from its global minimum or whether the iterates settle at a point, each question takes a separate argument.

A bound of the form $Cq^T$, with $0<q<1$, shrinks by a fixed factor at each step. That’s linear convergence. Bounds of order $O(1/T)$ and $O(1/\sqrt T)$ decay more slowly and are sublinear. For a positive error sequence $e_t$, Q-superlinear convergence means $e_{t+1}/e_t\to0$, and Q-quadratic convergence requires $e_{t+1}\le Ce_t^2$ eventually. I find it simpler to compare consecutive errors directly. Once $e_t<1$, the real expression $\log\log e_t$ is undefined.

When I count steps below, I’m asking how many it takes to guarantee an error within $\epsilon$. Change the function or where you start and the constants change too.

0x02 Smooth Strongly Convex with GD#

defination

Let's start with the case where we have both assumptions. Suppose $f$ is $L$-smooth and $\mu$-strongly convex, and keep the step size fixed with $0<\eta\le1/L$. The bound is as follows.
$$ f(w_T)-f^*\le(1-\eta\mu)^T(f(w_0)-f^*). $$

Let’s try one step. Put $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla f(w_t)$ into the descent lemma to see how much the function value drops. We have a squared gradient left, so the next move is to relate it to the function-value gap.

$$ \begin{aligned} f(w_{t+1})-f(w_t) &\le-\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2\\ &\le-\frac\eta2\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2 \le-\eta\mu(f(w_t)-f^*). \end{aligned} $$

PL connects the two quantities on the last line. Subtract the minimum value on both sides and we have a one-step recursion we can repeat $T$ times.

Back to the question we started with. How many steps? Take $\eta=1/L$ and start with $\mu<L$. Write $E_0=f(w_0)-f^{\ast}$ and bound the power by an exponential to get $f(w_T)-f^{\ast}\le e^{-\mu T/L}E_0$. If the initial error is above the tolerance, $T\ge(L/\mu)\log(E_0/\epsilon)$ is enough. The condition number $L/\mu$ sets the factor by which the bound shrinks. For $\mu=L$, the original recursion gives a zero bound in one step.

0x03 Smooth Convex with GD#

defination

Next keep convexity and smoothness. If $f$ is convex and $L$-smooth, choose $\eta=1/L$. The bound below holds for $T\ge1$.
$$ f(w_T)-f^*\le\frac{L\|w_0-w^*\|^2}{2T}. $$

If you don’t see how to use the descent lemma yet, try starting with the distance $R_t=\Vert w_t-w^{\ast}\Vert$. Convexity puts an inner product above the objective gap. Go back to the squared-distance expansion in the previous post and use it to rewrite that inner product.

$$ f(w_t)-f^*\le\langle\nabla f(w_t),w_t-w^*\rangle =\frac{R_t^2-R_{t+1}^2}{2\eta}+\frac\eta2\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2. $$

Why choose $\eta=1/L$ here? After adding the descent lemma, find the two squared-gradient terms. One comes in positive and one negative. This step size makes them cancel.

$$ f(w_{t+1})-f^*\le\frac L2(R_t^2-R_{t+1}^2). $$

Try adding the inequalities from $t=0$ to $T-1$. Each intermediate squared distance cancels with the one next to it, giving $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(f(w_{t+1})-f^{\ast})\le LR_0^2/2$. The function value is nonincreasing at this step size, so every term in the sum is at least as large as the last. The sum is therefore at least $T(f(w_T)-f^{\ast})$, giving a sublinear iteration complexity of $O(LR_0^2/\epsilon)$ for the last iterate.

0x04 Non-smooth Convex with Subgradient Method#

For a non-smooth objective we switch to subgradients and go back to the squared distance to an optimum. Assume $f$ is convex and use the update below.

$$ w_{t+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal C}(w_t-\eta_tg_t),\qquad g_t\in\partial f(w_t),\quad\|g_t\|\le G. $$

Here $\Pi_{\mathcal C}$ is Euclidean projection, which brings the parameter back when there’s a constraint. In the unconstrained case, take the subgradient step by itself. The assumption we need here is a bound on the subgradient selected at each step.

Fixed Step Size for a Given Horizon#

Suppose you’ve set the number of iterations in advance, with $T\ge1$, and write the initial distance as $R=\Vert w_0-w^{\ast}\Vert>0$. We can use $\eta=R/(G\sqrt T)$ throughout. This step size is hard to guess at first. Sum the distance inequalities below and look at the two terms that survive.

$$ \min_{0\le t<T}(f(w_t)-f^*)\le\frac{GR}{\sqrt T}. $$

In practice we often have only an upper bound on the starting distance, which also works for setting the step size. If the starting point is already optimal, we can stop there.

Start with the projection. It can only decrease or preserve the distance to $w^{\ast}\in\mathcal C$, so expanding the squared distance before projection gives us an upper bound.

$$ R_{t+1}^2\le R_t^2-2\eta(f(w_t)-f^*)+\eta^2G^2. $$

Divide the sum by $2\eta T$ and you can see what the step size is doing. A bigger step makes the first term smaller and the second bigger. Our choice makes the two terms equal.

$$ \min_{0\le t<T}(f(w_t)-f^*) \le\frac{R^2}{2\eta T}+\frac{\eta G^2}{2} =\frac{GR}{\sqrt T}. $$

So $T\ge G^2R^2/\epsilon^2$ steps are enough. Here we pick the point with the smallest function value among the iterates. Jensen gives the same bound for their equally weighted average. The last iterate would need a separate argument.

Polyak Step Size#

If you’re wondering where this step size came from, I find it easier to work backward. Suppose we know $f^{\ast}$ and the current point still has a gap. Leave the step size as an unknown in the one-step distance bound, then minimize that quadratic. The fraction below drops out.

$$ \eta_t=\frac{f(w_t)-f^*}{\|g_t\|^2}. $$

To substitute the step size, first use convexity to get $\langle g_t,w_t-w^{\ast}\rangle\ge f(w_t)-f^{\ast}$. The inner product has a negative coefficient in the distance expansion, which lets us keep the function-value gap in the bound.

$$ R_{t+1}^2\le R_t^2-\frac{(f(w_t)-f^*)^2}{\|g_t\|^2}. $$

Now sum and apply $\Vert g_t\Vert\le G$ to get $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(f(w_t)-f^{\ast})^2\le G^2R^2$, so the best function-value gap is again at most $GR/\sqrt T$. If $g_t=0$, convexity tells us the point is optimal and we can stop. This rule needs $f^{\ast}$, so it applies when the minimum value is already known.

0x05 Smooth Non-convex with GD#

For a non-convex function, let’s ask something we can compute directly. Do we pass through a point with a small gradient along the way? Assume $f$ is $L$-smooth with a finite lower bound $f_{\inf}$. Keep $\eta=1/L$ and start with the decrease in one step.

$$ f(w_{t+1})\le f(w_t)-\frac1{2L}\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2. $$

Sum from $t=0$ through $T-1$. The decrease at each step connects to the next, so all we need on the right at the end is a lower bound on the function value.

$$ \frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla f(w_t)\|^2 \le\frac{2L(f(w_0)-f_{\inf})}{T}. $$

Here’s a detail that’s easy to read too quickly. We bounded the average squared gradient norm, so at least one point has a squared norm that small. Targeting $\Vert\nabla f\Vert^2\le\epsilon$ takes $O(1/\epsilon)$ steps. If you want $\Vert\nabla f\Vert\le\epsilon$, square the tolerance before you plug it in, giving $O(1/\epsilon^2)$. Both are stationarity measures.

0x06 Strongly Convex and Non-smooth with Subgradient Method#

That leaves the strongly convex, non-smooth case. Suppose $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex on the closed convex set $\mathcal C$, with a minimizer attained in $\mathcal C$. As before, impose $\Vert g_t\Vert\le G$ along the projected subgradient iterates, where the bound is needed.

This time the step size changes with the iteration. Take $\eta_t=2/(\mu(t+2))$. Putting it into the recursion gives the following bound for $T\ge1$.

$$ \min_{0\le t<T}(f(w_t)-f^*)\le\frac{2G^2}{\mu(T+1)}. $$

Start with the squared distance again. Using the strong-convexity subgradient inequality on the inner product gives us an extra decrease term. The projection step is the same.

$$ R_{t+1}^2\le(1-\mu\eta_t)R_t^2-2\eta_t(f(w_t)-f^*)+\eta_t^2G^2. $$

After substituting the step size, look at the two distance terms on the right. Their coefficients differ by two, so consecutive terms don’t line up if we sum them as they stand.

$$ f(w_t)-f^*\le\frac\mu4\left[tR_t^2-(t+2)R_{t+1}^2\right]+\frac{G^2}{\mu(t+2)}. $$

I multiply by $t+1$ and write out the next step beside it. Compare the two lines. The matching distance terms now have the same coefficients with opposite signs, so they cancel when we sum.

$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(t+1)(f(w_t)-f^*) &\le-\frac\mu4T(T+1)R_T^2 +\frac{G^2}{\mu}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{t+1}{t+2}\\ &\le\frac{TG^2}{\mu}. \end{aligned} $$

Now divide by the total weight $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(t+1)=T(T+1)/2$. Jensen gives the function-value bound for the average with the same weights. The rate is $O(1/T)$, which is sublinear.

I keep the cases in one table so I can find them next time. I pay particular attention to the strongly convex, non-smooth row. Its rate is the one we got from the weighted sum. If you’re using a row for your own problem, check the step size, lower bound, minimizer, and subgradient bound. For a non-smooth, non-convex problem we need a different algorithm and stationarity measure.
